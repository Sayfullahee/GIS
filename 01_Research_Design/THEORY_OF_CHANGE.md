## 1. Institutional Mandate & Context

The National Hydroelectric Power Producing Areas Development Commission (N-HYPPADEC) is statutorily mandated under Nigerian law to manage environmental, ecological, and socio-economic challenges resulting from the construction, expansion, and operations of major hydroelectric power facilities. The Commission's jurisdiction spans ten confirmed member states: **Niger (`NG-NIG`), Kebbi (`NG-KEB`), Plateau (`NG-PLA`), Benue (`NG-BEN`), Kogi (`NG-KOG`), Kwara (`NG-KWA`), Kaduna (`NG-KAD`), Nasarawa (`NG-NAS`), Taraba (`NG-TAR`), and Gombe (`NG-GOM`)**.

While these riverine corridors generate more than 70% of Nigeria's grid power (via the Kainji, Jebba, Shiroro, Dadin Kowa, Kashimbila, and Zungeru facilities), host communities endure chronic compound vulnerabilities:
- Upstream pulse discharges and uncoordinated seasonal dam spillage.
- Severe riverbank scour, agricultural topsoil stripping, and headward gully incisions.
- Chronic local electricity supply and distribution failures despite power proximity.
- Compounded climate variability (prolonged dry spells, intense localized convective storms).
- Displacement, loss of fertile alluvial farmlands, and restricted access to markets.

The **CHER-CDSS Theory of Change** provides the causal architecture connecting scientific monitoring to targeted engineering, ecological, and policy interventions.

## 2. Conceptual Causal Architecture

The Theory of Change operates along a seven-tier causal progression:

```
[ Tier 1: Pressures ]
  Climate Variability + Upstream Dam Operations + Watershed Deforestation + Demographic Expansion
         │
         ▼
[ Tier 2: Hazards ]
  Riverine & Pulse Floods (G24/G25) + Gully/Bank Erosion (G26/G27) + Hydro Disruption (G28)
         │
         ▼
[ Tier 3: Exposure ]
  Riparian Settlements + Alluvial Farmlands + Critical Facilities + Transport Corridors (G29/G30)
         │
         ▼
[ Tier 4: Vulnerability & Fragility ]
  Subsistence Livelihoods + Inadequate Shelter + Low Adaptive Capacity + Energy Poverty (G33)
         │
         ▼
[ Tier 5: Direct Impacts ]
  Displacement + Crop/Livestock Loss + Asset Washouts + Disease Outbreaks + Conflict Triggers
         │
         ▼
[ Tier 6: N-HYPPADEC Interventions ]
  Flood Dykes + Gabion Check Dams + Resilient Water/Boreholes + Early Warning Telemetry (G36)
         │
         ▼
[ Tier 7: Resilience Outcomes & MRV ]
  Reduced Hazard Impact (IES > 0) + Preserved Assets + Stabilized Livelihoods + Residual Risk Managed (G37)
```

## 3. Tier-by-Tier Narrative

### Tier 1: Systemic Pressures & Drivers
- **Climatic Drivers:** Increased frequency of extreme precipitation events, delayed onset followed by concentrated rainfall, and warming temperatures altering regional evapotranspiration.
- **Hydrological Alterations:** Artificial hydrographs created by peak turbine generation and emergency flood-gate spillages from Kainji, Jebba, Shiroro, and Dadin Kowa.
- **Land-Cover Degradation:** Loss of riparian buffer vegetation, unsustainable wetland drainage, artisanal mining pits (Plateau), and excessive livestock compaction along riverbanks.

### Tier 2: Physical Hazards ($H$)
- **Flood Hazards:** Deep seasonal inundation in low-gradient valleys (Niger, Kebbi, Benue), flash flooding in urban/peri-urban rivers (Kaduna, Makurdi), and tributary backwater surge at confluence zones (Lokoja).
- **Erosion Hazards:** Active headward gully progression into settlements, lateral riverbank retreat threatening structures, and sheet erosion stripping fertile topsoil.
- **Operational Hazards:** Hydro supply disruptions, dam maintenance outages, and localized transmission line failures.

### Tier 3: Physical & Human Exposure ($E$)
- Spatial overlay of hazard polygons with physical assets:
  - 50 surveyed sentinel communities containing over 223,000 residents in direct hazard zones.
  - Over 45,000 residential and commercial structures mapped within 500m of active river channels or critical gullies.
  - Critical access bridges, culverts, health clinics, and rural markets.

### Tier 4: Vulnerability & Adaptive Capacity ($V$)
- High sensitivity driven by primary dependence on rain-fed subsistence agriculture and artisanal fishing.
- Low household asset buffers, limited access to formal credit or disaster insurance.
- Adaptation deficit: Only 28.4% of surveyed riparian communities have structured flood protection dykes or operational community early warning sirens.

### Tier 5: Direct Socioeconomic Impacts
- Acute crop washouts during harvest periods leading to household food consumption deficits (Food Consumption Score drops).
- Out-migration and displacement into temporary camps or peripheral urban slums.
- Secondary infrastructure destruction cutting off market corridors and escalating localized resource conflicts.

### Tier 6: Commission Interventions (Capital & Programmatic)
- **Hard Engineering:** Reinforced concrete floodwalls, riverbank rip-rap and gabion revetments, retention ponds, hydraulic culverts, and semi-arid check dams.
- **Nature-Based Solutions (NbS):** Riparian agroforestry buffer strips, vetiver grass contour plantings, and watershed soil conservation.
- **Resilience Infrastructure:** Solar-powered high-yield boreholes, elevated flood evacuation shelters, and community radio warning sirens.
- **Livelihood Support:** Flood-tolerant seed varieties, artisanal fishing equipment, and micro-grant rehabilitation packages.

### Tier 7: Resilience Outcomes, MRV & Residual Risk
- System measurement of the **Intervention Effectiveness Score (IES)**:
  $$IES = \frac{R_{\text{before}} - R_{\text{after}}}{R_{\text{before}}} \times 100\%$$
- Tracking **Residual Risk** ($R_{\text{after}}$) to ensure ongoing institutional contingency and maintenance funding.
- Re-classification of priority tiers from Critical (Tier 1) down to High (Tier 2), Moderate (Tier 3), and Ongoing Monitoring (Tier 4).

## 4. Key Assumptions & Boundary Conditions

1. **Hydrological Cooperation:** Hydropower operators (Mainstream Energy, North-South Power, etc.) and Federal Ministry of Water Resources provide timely reservoir telemetry and spillway alerts.
2. **Community Stewardship:** Local disaster response committees actively maintain early warning telemetry, boreholes, and vegetative contour buffers.
3. **Budgetary Allocation Continuity:** N-HYPPADEC capital allocations are directed strictly via the objective empirical rankings of the Top-20 Priority Communities Register.
4. **Data Integrity:** Enumerators, supervisors, and GIS analysts adhere to the non-negotiable scientific and ethical protocols codified in Scaffold v4.1 Section 41.
